# Landing page

Use for a page organized around one offer, audience, and primary action: product launches, campaigns, trials, demos, or waitlists. A general homepage with several equal visitor intents does not automatically qualify. Pricing comparison belongs here when it supports that offer.

## Establish the argument
Read supplied product facts, existing content, brand assets, and conversion behavior first. Identify the offer, intended audience, traffic source, visitor knowledge, primary action, objections, available evidence, and actual commercial terms. Ask together for missing facts that change the offer or action; label reversible design assumptions. Never invent customer logos, testimonials, numerical results, scarcity, or guarantees.

Choose structure from intent: a classic product demonstration for an understandable offer; a narrative for unfamiliar or high-friction decisions; a minimal page for high-intent visitors; a comparison for visitors evaluating alternatives. Explain the choice. These are strategies, not mandatory section inventories.

## Compose and write
- Let the hero answer what it is, whom it helps, why it matters, and what happens next. Match the arrival promise; lead with a specific benefit supported by supplied facts.
- Place evidence beside its claim. With no proof, show an honest product demonstration or acknowledge evidence needed in the design contract; do not disguise invented numbers as realistic data.
- Prioritize one action while allowing subordinate navigation and necessary alternatives. Repeat its meaning consistently; a CTA must name the next step and reach it.
- Arrange benefits, explanation, proof, and objection handling according to the visitor's uncertainty. Add FAQs and risk reversal only when real questions and supported terms warrant them.
- For pricing, state currency, billing period, full billing commitment, meaningful limits, and differences. A billing toggle updates all dependent amounts and labels. Explain a recommended plan through fit, not an unsupported popularity claim.
- Derive composition, imagery, and typography from the product and brand. Demonstrations, editorial scale changes, or motion must communicate something; do not require a font, gradient heading, floating navigation, or reveal sequence.

## Handoff and acceptance
Add the outline, hero/CTA copy, evidence mapping, layout rationale, pricing assumptions, responsive priorities, and interaction states to the parent design contract. Recommend indexing from search intent and campaign requirements, not from URL shape; identify title, description, and social-preview needs without promising search placement.

Walk the primary action and pricing choices on mobile and desktop. Verify promised versus actual next step, missing proof handling, keyboard/forms/error feedback, and readable content without motion. Flag unresolved offer or destination facts before claiming a production-ready page.
