# Blog

Use for publication indexes, article pages, categories, and publication search. Writing an article without designing its reading surface belongs to the content task, not this subskill.

## Establish reading and discovery
Inspect real content shapes, audience, publication identity, taxonomy, author/date metadata, media, and current navigation. Identify whether visitors browse, search for an answer, or arrive directly at an article. Ask only when missing information changes hierarchy or navigation. Do not invent author credentials, dates, quotations, or sources.

## Design the flow
- Give the index an editorial hierarchy: feature the work that deserves attention, then make topic, title, summary, and provenance scannable. Do not make every story compete as an identical card.
- Categories and search need visible active scope, usable result titles, result counts when known, and a recoverable no-results state. Preserve query and category when returning from an article.
- Establish article rhythm with a clear title, useful introduction, descriptive headings, comfortable reading measure, and captions adjacent to media. Choose typography for sustained reading and brand character, not a universal font whitelist.
- Provide contents navigation when it helps a long article; keep anchor targets clear of sticky UI. Related reading belongs after or beside the argument without interrupting it. Reading progress is optional and must not imply completion tracking.
- Distinguish tutorials, essays, news, and visual stories through content needs. Multimedia can explain the subject; the essential reading path must remain available without animation.
- On small screens, preserve hierarchy and reading order. Contain wide tables/code locally rather than widening the page; keep media explanations and links accessible.
- Subscription or promotional controls must explain their effect and leave reading available. In a local example, explicitly report that nothing was subscribed or sent.

## Handoff and acceptance
Return index/article hierarchy, discovery behavior, content metadata, responsive reading rules, and empty/error states through the parent contract. Include source-backed article title, author, publication/update dates, and optional structured-data recommendations. [Google's Article documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/article) owns current markup guidance; accurate metadata does not guarantee enhanced search results.

Verify search plus category interaction, no results/reset, article navigation and return context, keyboard/focus, long headings, rich-content overflow, and mobile reading. Inspect an actual long article, not just its hero.
